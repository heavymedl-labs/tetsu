# Build one shell, three homes, one account

Research on 2026-11-22, extending the [signed-in experience](signed-in-experience.md) note: six research passes over
Linear, general SaaS, booking software for gyms and salons, accessible app shells, account-area security, and first
dashboards, plus two passes through a live, throwaway Linear workspace at desktop, tablet, and phone widths. Its
screenshots are not kept, since the repository is public.

Tetsu should build **one server-rendered shell shared by all three realms**: a skip link, a header with the realm's context at left and the person's initials menu at right (the #148 header), a sidebar of plain links that becomes a settings sidebar with "Back to app" when the person opens their account, and one `main`. Each realm then fills it differently. Operators get a sidebar console whose home is a queue of what needs them. Staff get the same sidebar plus a business switcher, a "today" home filtered by role, and business settings gated by role. Customers, whose account belongs to one business, get a lighter top-navigation shell whose home leads with their next booking. Every realm's personal account has the same pages in Linear's order: Profile, then Security (passkeys and sessions), then Notifications and Preferences when there is something to set. The evidence is strong on shape: Linear, Vercel, GitHub, Stripe, Fresha and Square all split "me" settings from "the organization" settings, and booking products all open staff on today's schedule. It is strong on security too: OWASP ASVS 5.0 Level 2 requires visible logout on every authenticated page, a self-service session list, and re-authentication before sensitive changes, all of which this account area carries. Linear's shape is worth copying but its accessibility details are not: live inspection found an unlabelled `nav`, the current page marked only by a data attribute, and a phone-width sidebar that opens without moving focus. The evidence is weak on per-role sidebar visibility and the customer homes of Mindbody, Fresha and Vagaro, so those recommendations rest on inference. The practical result is a build order that starts with #148's header as a shared template, turns the operator's `/ops` card into a two-page account area and a real home, and then reuses all of it for staff and customers in their sign-in slices.

## Linear runs one shell in two modes, app and settings

Linear's web app, observed in a live throwaway workspace on 2026-11-22, is a two-column shell: a left sidebar and a main view in a rounded panel, with a thin status strip at the bottom (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). The sidebar runs from personal to shared. At the top is the workspace menu (initials avatar, workspace name, chevron) with search and new-issue buttons beside it. Below come Inbox (with an unread count), My issues, Reviews, Documents and Agent, then a collapsible "Workspace" group (Projects, Views and more), then "Your teams", each team expanding into Home, Issues, Projects and Views, and in a new account a "Try" group of onboarding actions. Linear's docs confirm the logic: My issues sits "in the top-left sidebar under Inbox" ([Linear: My issues](https://linear.app/docs/my-issues)), Favorites appears only after the first favorite exists ([Linear: Favorites](https://linear.app/docs/favorites.md)), and users can reorder, hide or move items behind **More** ([Linear changelog 2024-12-18](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-12-18-personalized-sidebar)). The 2024 redesign names the sidebar and top header together an "inverted L", "the global chrome of the application that controls the content in the main view" ([Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)), and the March 2026 refresh dimmed the sidebar so the main view stands out and made headers consistent across views ([Linear changelog 2026-03-12](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-12-ui-refresh)).

**Linear has no avatar menu at the top right.** The live workspace menu at the top left holds Settings, Invite and manage members, Switch workspace (with "Create or join a workspace"), and Log out (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). Linear's login docs still mention an account menu "in the upper right" for adding a second email ([Linear: Login methods](https://linear.app/docs/login-methods)); the live app did not show one, so the docs appear to lag the product. One menu works for Linear because its people live in one workspace at a time. It works less well where a person has several contexts, which is why Fresha and Vercel keep the person's own menu apart from the switcher, and why Tetsu's #148 header puts the person at the right.

Settings is a mode, not a page. Choosing Settings opens `linear.app/<workspace>/settings`, which lands on `/settings/account/preferences`, and replaces the app sidebar with a settings sidebar topped by "Back to app" and a search field (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). The docs describe four groups since December 2024: **Account** (personal), **Features**, **Administration** (admins only) and **Your teams** ([Linear changelog 2024-12-18](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-12-18-personalized-sidebar)). The live app labels the first group "Personal" and splits Features into Issues, Projects, Features and Integrations. Members see only their own settings and their teams' settings, admins and owners also see Administration, and guests "can only access their own Account settings tab" ([Linear: Members and roles](https://linear.app/docs/members-roles); [Linear: Workspaces](https://linear.app/docs/workspaces)). Visibility works by whole group, not by greying out pages.

| Group (live) | Pages, under `linear.app/<workspace>/settings` |
| --- | --- |
| Personal | Preferences `/account/preferences`, Profile `/account/profile`, Notifications `/account/notifications`, Code & reviews, Security & access `/account/security`, Connected accounts `/account/connections`, Agent personalization |
| Issues, Projects, Features, Integrations | Labels, templates, statuses, SLAs, feature toggles, `/integrations` |
| Administration | Workspace `/workspace`, Teams `/teams`, Members `/members`, Permissions `/permissions`, Security `/security`, API `/api`, Applications, Billing `/billing`, Usage, Import & export |

The URLs settle a conflict in the sources. Linear's docs give workspace-less paths such as `linear.app/settings/account/profile` ([Linear: Profile](https://linear.app/docs/profile)), but the live app carries the workspace slug in every settings URL. The live observation is the current behavior.

Each settings page is one centered column, about 740px wide, of titled sections, each with a one-line description and a rounded card of rows: a label, a sentence beneath it, and a control at the right. Personal pages split three concerns. **Preferences** covers how the app behaves for me (default home view, display names, first day of week, theme). **Profile** covers who I am (picture defaulting to initials, read-only email, full name, title, username, and "Leave workspace"). **Security & access** covers how I get in and what can act as me: a Sessions list where each row shows "Chrome on Linux", a green "Current session" marker and a city, region and country, with Log out on others; Passkeys with "No passkeys registered" and a "New passkey" button; then personal API keys, a signing key and authorized applications. The docs add that inactive sessions expire after 30 days and that "Revoke all" ends every session but the current one ([Linear: Security & access](https://linear.app/docs/security-and-access)). The Members page is a table of Name, Email, Role, Teams, Joined and Last seen, with Export CSV and Invite. Plan-gated rows stay listed with "Available on Enterprise" rather than hidden.

On desktop the sidebar is one `nav`, 244px wide, of links 28px tall with 8px rounded corners and a 150ms color transition; the current item gets a subtle filled background. A skip link and one `main` exist. Ctrl+K opens a centered command menu with focus in a "Type a command or search..." field, results grouped under headings, each command's shortcut at the right (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). The workspace menu likewise shows each item's shortcut.

Below about 768px Linear keeps the sidebar in the page but moves it off-screen. A second live pass at 390px wide found the header reduced to a "Menu" button at the far left, the view's name, and its controls at the right (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). "Menu" slides the sidebar, about 330px wide, in from the left over the content with a **40% black backdrop**, leaving about 60px of the page visible; tapping the backdrop or pressing "Menu" again closes it (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). The motion is short, CSS transitions of 150 to 200ms with ease-out. Settings does the same with its own sidebar: the header holds the same "Menu" button and a "Settings" link with a back chevron to the app, the cards fill the width, and "Menu" slides in "Back to app", search and the settings groups with the current page highlighted (observed in a live workspace on 2026-11-22). On native mobile Linear chose a different model, a user-arranged bottom toolbar plus pins ([Linear changelog 2026-01-22](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-01-22-customize-your-navigation-in-linear-mobile)). A fresh Linear account opened on an "Agent" view with a banner offering to change the default, and users choose their own "Default home view" ([Linear: Preferences](https://linear.app/docs/account-preferences)). Linear documents no first-run tour inside the app.

## Peers agree: the avatar holds "me", the switcher holds "us"

Across general SaaS, **personal settings and organization settings are separate areas with separate entry points**. Vercel opens personal settings from the profile picture at the top right, at `vercel.com/account/settings` and `/account/authentication` (where passkeys sit in one list with other login methods), while team settings live in the team's own sidebar under `vercel.com/<team>/...` ([Vercel: Account Management](https://vercel.com/docs/accounts)). GitHub does the same from the avatar, with personal **Settings > Access > Sessions** and **Settings > Archives > Security log** ([GitHub: sessions](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/viewing-and-managing-your-sessions); [GitHub: security log](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/reviewing-your-security-log)). Stripe is the outlier: one settings tree with Personal, Account and Product groups, where personal details sit at `/settings/user` inside the account-scoped tree ([Stripe: Web Dashboard](https://docs.stripe.com/dashboard/basics)). Notion likewise reaches the person's page through the workspace menu ([Notion: Account settings](https://www.notion.com/help/account-settings)). The role rule that falls out of these products is that the personal area looks the same for every role, while the organization area hides sections by role.

Switching follows two models. In the **scoped-dashboard model**, one organization is active and the whole interface follows it: Vercel's team switcher at the top left with a remembered default team shown "whenever you first log in" ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/accounts)), Stripe's account name in the upper left ([Stripe: Multiple accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/multiple-accounts)), and Slack's workspace icon ([Slack: Switch workspaces](https://slack.com/help/articles/1500002200741-Switch-between-workspaces)). In the **global-identity model**, GitHub keeps navigation personal and visits organizations as pages. Vercel also solves deep links across teams with a redirect, `vercel.com/d?to=/[team]/~/settings/billing`, which fills in the current team ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/accounts)), a device worth copying for emails and help pages that cannot know which business a person uses.

Booking software for gyms and salons follows the same split. **Fresha** documents it most cleanly: a team member's own contact details are changed "only by that team member", from the profile picture at the top right through My Profile > Personal Settings ([Fresha: Update team member details](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/team/583-update-team-member-account-details)); personal language, appearance and notifications affect only that person, and managers cannot change another member's notification choices ([Fresha: Manage your personal account](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/personal-account/103372-manage-your-personal-account)). Square separates Personal information > Sign in & security from My business > Security ([Square: 2-Step Verification](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5593)). Mindbody moved to a collapsible left sidebar with the most used pages first and renamed "Manager Tools" to "Settings" ([Mindbody: updated navigation](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/education/blog/whats-new-your-updated-navigation-and-more)). For people at several businesses, Fresha's model is the closest match to Tetsu's staff realm: one login reaches every workspace for that email, a switcher sits in the top toolbar, each workspace has its own calendar, clients and settings, and **a person's role can differ per workspace** ([Fresha: Switch between workspaces](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/workspace-settings/101696-switch-between-workspaces)). Losing context on a switch is a recurring complaint: Square's apps log a person out when they pick a location where they are not active ([Square: multiple locations](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5834-square-appointments-for-multiple-locations)), and a Boulevard reviewer said the app "resets every time" stores are switched ([Capterra: Boulevard](https://capterra.com/p/180087/Boulevard/reviews/)).

Customer portals split on scope. Marketplaces give one platform login over a per-business client record. Square instead scopes a customer account to one seller, shows past and upcoming appointments, cards on file and rebooking, signs in by phone code, and shows only one seller's appointments at a time ([Square: client profile](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6439-manage-your-client-profile-with-square-appointments)). Tetsu's ADR-0040 already chose the Square shape: a customer account belongs to one business and the same person at two businesses has two unrelated accounts ([ADR-0040](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/blob/main/docs/adr/0040-identity-and-sign-in.md)). The cost of that choice shows in Glofox reviews, where people with several gyms had to log out to switch and the developer confirmed one gym per login ([Glofox on the App Store](https://apple.co/3KYj3Gd)). Passkeys soften it, because the browser offers each account's passkey by name, so a customer switches by signing in on another business's page rather than by resetting a password. Fresha's account deletion rule also maps across: owners and staff must leave or close every workspace before deleting their personal account ([Fresha: Delete your personal account](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/personal-account/237-delete-your-personal-account)).

## Accessible shells are lists of links, not ARIA menus

Design systems agree on the shell's skeleton: a banner header, a `nav` landmark holding a plain list of real links with the current one marked by `aria-current`, and one `main`. GOV.UK orders header content from most general to most specific, puts account and sign-out links in the navigation's end slot, and asks for "Skip to main content" ([GOV.UK: Navigate a service](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/navigate-a-service/); [GOV.UK: Service navigation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/service-navigation/)). Primer's NavList renders a `nav` of links with `aria-current="page"`, groups long lists under headings, and recommends a heading that names the `nav` on settings-style pages ([Primer: NavList](https://primer.style/product/components/nav-list/)). Primer's PageLayout maps header, content and footer to landmarks and offers a full-screen sidebar below 768px that covers the viewport like a dialog ([Primer: PageLayout](https://primer.style/product/components/page-layout/)). Atlassian's public bug reports record what goes wrong otherwise: a side navigation landmark without a unique label and items keyboard users could not reach ([JRASERVER-74192](https://jira.atlassian.com/browse/JRASERVER-74192)). Tailwind Plus, which Tetsu already draws from, gets the layout right (a fixed sidebar at `lg` and up, a native `<dialog>` below it) but ships no `aria-current`, no skip link and two unlabelled `nav` copies, so it is a styling starting point, not an accessibility reference (local file `tailwindplus/ui-blocks/.../Sidebar Layouts/Simple sidebar/v4/html-light.html`).

WCAG 2.2 AA shapes the shell through a handful of criteria. **2.4.1 Bypass Blocks** requires the skip link. **1.3.1 Info and Relationships** requires landmarks, lists and a programmatic current item. **1.4.10 Reflow** requires the page to work at 320 CSS px, which equals 1280px at 400% zoom, so the narrow layout must be fully keyboard operable, not just touch friendly ([Understanding 1.4.10](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)). **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured** fails a sticky header that fully hides a focused field, and CSS `scroll-padding` is a sufficient technique ([Understanding 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). **2.5.8 Target Size** sets 24 by 24 CSS px for icon-only buttons such as the menu toggle and the initials ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). **3.2.3 Consistent Navigation** and **3.2.6 Consistent Help** require repeated navigation and help in the same relative order on every page ([Understanding 3.2.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/consistent-help.html)), which is the strongest argument for one shared shell template rather than per-realm copies.

The account menu should not be an ARIA menu. The APG's disclosure navigation example "does not use the WAI-ARIA menu role" because site navigation lacks the behavior assistive technologies expect from a menu ([APG: disclosure navigation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/examples/disclosure-navigation/)), while a true menu button commits the page to arrow keys, type-ahead and strict role nesting ([APG: menu button](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/menu-button/)). Adrian Roselli's practitioner view is blunter: menu roles announce desktop-application semantics and small mistakes break navigation, so use `nav`, real links and disclosure buttons ([Roselli](https://adrianroselli.com/2017/10/dont-use-aria-menu-roles-for-site-nav.html)). The APG still lists a navigation menubar example, so this is a consensus of practice rather than a spec rule. For the initials menu, a button with `aria-expanded` that reveals links (Account) and a sign-out `<form method="post">` is the robust choice; Escape closes it and returns focus to the button.

Linear itself shows what to copy and what to skip. Its look and motion are sound: 28px items clear the 24px target minimum, and transitions of 150 to 200ms stay brief. Its semantics fall short of every pattern above. The current page is marked `data-active="true"` and `data-current-page="true"` rather than `aria-current`, so a screen reader hears no current item (1.3.1). The sidebar `nav` has no label. At phone width the "Menu" button has `aria-label` but no `aria-expanded`, opening the sidebar leaves focus on the page body, and the hidden sidebar is neither `inert` nor `aria-hidden`, so keyboard and screen reader users can tab through links they cannot see. Escape did not close it in the test, though the key may not have reached the test frame. The workspace menu declares `aria-haspopup="menu"` and `aria-expanded`, which commits Linear to the full menu keyboard model, and the Ctrl+K command menu has no `dialog`, `aria-modal`, combobox or listbox roles (Linear live observation, 2026-11-22, in `linear_interactions.md`). Linear's off-canvas shape is worth copying; its markup is not.

Working without JavaScript follows from the same choice. GOV.UK ships its mobile "Menu" button with the `hidden` attribute, so without script the button is absent and the links stay visible ([GOV.UK: Service navigation](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/service-navigation/)). Tetsu's version is: render the sidebar once in normal flow, so on a narrow screen without script it stacks above `main` and the skip link jumps past it; let a small island reveal the menu button and move the same `nav` into a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, which gives inertness and Escape for free ([APG: modal dialog](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/)). The initials button is, without script, a link to the Account page that lists the same items. Settings pages suit per-section forms, each with its own Save button, which Tailwind Plus's settings screens already use, and destructive actions sit last with a confirmation page that a dialog later upgrades ([Shopify: settings template](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/app-home/patterns/templates/settings)). Invoker commands (`command`, `commandfor`) would cut script further, but their browser support in 2026 was not checked against Tetsu's supported browsers.

## ASVS Level 2 fixes what the Security page must hold

The account area is where most of Tetsu's session and authenticator requirements become visible, and at ASVS 5.0 Level 2 several are not optional. **v5.0.0-7.4.4 (L2)** requires "easy and visible access to logout functionality" on every authenticated page, which #148's header satisfies. **v5.0.0-7.5.2 (L2)** requires that users can view and, "having authenticated again with at least one factor", terminate any or all sessions. **v5.0.0-7.5.1 (L2)** requires full re-authentication before changing attributes that affect authentication, such as email or recovery settings. **v5.0.0-7.4.3 (L2)** requires the option to end all other sessions after any authenticator change, and **v5.0.0-7.4.2 (L1)** requires ending all sessions when an account is disabled or deleted ([ASVS V7](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x16-V7-Session-Management.md)). State-changing actions such as ending a session, deleting a passkey or deleting an account must be POST with CSRF defense (**v5.0.0-3.5.1, 3.5.3**), pages must forbid framing (**3.4.6**), and user-agent-derived device labels and passkey names must be rendered as text (**3.2.2**) ([ASVS V3](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x12-V3-Web-Frontend-Security.md)). Account pages need `Cache-Control: no-store` (**v5.0.0-14.3.2**) ([ASVS V14](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x23-V14-Data-Protection.md)), a person must reach only their own sessions and passkeys, and tenants must never touch each other (**v5.0.0-8.2.1, 8.2.2, 8.4.1**) ([ASVS V8](https://github.com/OWASP/ASVS/blob/v5.0.0/5.0/en/0x17-V8-Authorization.md)). Tetsu's ADR-0059 already meets 7.5.2 strictly: ending sessions needs a fresh passkey login within ten minutes, the list shows start, last request, a coarse device label and the current session, and adding or removing a passkey ends other sessions without asking ([ADR-0059](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/blob/main/docs/adr/0059-session-controls-and-request-checks.md)).

NIST SP 800-63B-4 adds duties ASVS leaves at Level 3. It requires that multiple authenticators can be bound (SHALL) and encourages at least two; it requires a notification through an independent channel whenever an authenticator is added and on every recovery (SHALL); it requires immediate invalidation of a lost authenticator (SHALL); it requires each notification to say what to do if the person did not make the change (SHALL); and it requires support for **at least two notification addresses** per account (SHALL) ([NIST SP 800-63B-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html)). At AAL2, which a user-verifying passkey reaches, sessions SHOULD last no more than 24 hours with 1 hour idle. The passkey list itself has settled conventions: **one card per passkey**, a creation prompt at the top when none exists ([Passkey Central](https://www.passkeycentral.org/design-user-journeys/required-patterns/create-view-and-manage-passkeys-in-account-settings)), and on each card the provider, created date, last used date and management actions ([Google: passkey UI](https://developers.google.com/identity/passkeys/ux/user-interface-design)). FIDO's removal pattern, read only through search snippets, adds a confirmation, a plain "Delete" label and a warning that names the fallback when the last passkey goes ([Passkey Central: remove passkeys](https://www.passkeycentral.org/design-guidelines/optional-patterns/remove-passkeys-from-service-provider-account-settings)). After a deletion or a name change, the WebAuthn Signal API lets password managers hide the dead credential or update the name ([Chrome: Signal API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/identity/webauthn-signal-api)). One dependency needs avoiding: the community AAGUID list that names passkey providers **returns an empty object from 6 January 2027**, so provider names must come from the FIDO Metadata Service or a fallback ([passkey-authenticator-aaguids](https://github.com/passkeydeveloper/passkey-authenticator-aaguids)).

OWASP's cheat sheets go further than ASVS in ways that fit a home page. They recommend an account activity "logbook" and showing "the date, time and location of their previous login attempt" at sign-in ([OWASP Credential Stuffing Prevention](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Credential_Stuffing_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html); [OWASP Session Management](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Session_Management_Cheat_Sheet.html)). GitHub's personal security log keeps 90 days and shows country rather than IP ([GitHub: security log](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/reviewing-your-security-log)), and Google's device list shows browser, approximate place and last contact ([Google Account Help](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3067630)). Location is where Tetsu should deliberately differ from Linear's city-level session rows. IP addresses are personal data within an access request ([EDPB Guidelines 01/2022](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-04/edpb_guidelines_202201_data_subject_rights_access_v2_en.pdf)), NIST requires fraud indicators such as geolocation to enter a privacy risk assessment, and ADR-0059 already stores only a coarse device label. A "previous sign-in: Tuesday, Firefox on Linux" line gives most of the detection value without a new data source.

For customers, the privacy duties sit with each business. Each business is the controller of its customers' data and Tetsu is its processor, which must help answer access, erasure and portability requests. The EDPB endorses a "Download your personal data" option "when logged into their user account" as a way to meet the right of access, accepts the account's own login as the verification, and warns that the tool must never narrow what is disclosed or replace other channels ([EDPB Guidelines 01/2022](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-04/edpb_guidelines_202201_data_subject_rights_access_v2_en.pdf)). Because a Tetsu customer account is already per business, a customer's "Your data" page (download, delete) is naturally scoped to one controller. The GDPR article wording in the notes was not read from EUR-Lex, so the exact Art. 15, 17 and 20 scope needs legal review before the page ships.

## Every useful home leads with today's work, not charts

Research separates a dashboard, "a visual display of the most important information... monitored at a glance" whose first pitfall is exceeding one screen ([UXmatters on Few](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2007/04/book-review-information-dashboard-design.php)), from a portal that routes people to tasks. NN/g says dashboards "communicate critical information" and are consumed fast, with operational dashboards for things to act on now ([NN/g: Dashboards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)). The products people praise put tasks first. **Shopify Home** lists open order tasks "organized by task" before four metrics, shows setup tasks to a new store, displays full metrics only after the first sale, and shows insight cards only once a store averages more than ten orders a week over six months ([Shopify Help: Shopify Home](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/shopify-admin/shopify-home)). Stripe Home pairs metrics with "important notifications, like unresolved disputes" ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/dashboard/basics)), and Linear's My issues orders work "in order of what you'd want to work on first" ([Linear: My issues](https://linear.app/docs/my-issues)). With little data, a task home beats a metrics home because a chart of zeros has no decision attached.

For **operators**, internal tools centre on the person's own queue. Zendesk Agent Home shows tickets assigned to the agent that gained a comment, rose in priority or were reassigned ([Zendesk: Agent Home](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5064623131418-Using-Agent-Home-to-manage-your-work-efficiently)), and agents pushed back when it hid the unassigned tickets in their groups ([Zendesk community](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/ko/community/posts/6745361237146-Add-List-of-Unassigned-Tickets-in-Your-Groups-to-Agent-Home?page=1)). An operator home should therefore show both "mine" and "unclaimed". Tetsu already records what such a queue needs: requests refused at a check, the operator's own sessions, and mail in the mailbox.

For **staff**, every booking product opens on today. **Vagaro Home**, released 15 September 2026, puts upcoming bookings "front and center", then booking requests, waitlist and action items, then revenue and commission, with an account setup checklist for the first 60 days; owners see the whole business while employees see their own schedule and earnings, because "the idea isn't to show you everything all the time" ([Vagaro: Introducing Vagaro Home](https://www.vagaro.com/learn/vagaro-home); [Vagaro update](https://www.vagaro.com/pro/updates/vagaro-home)). Glofox restricts a Trainer to their own classes while Admins see reporting ([Glofox: staff and permissions](https://support.glofox.com/hc/en-us/articles/46467475896340-Getting-Started-With-Staff-and-Permissions)), Square lets Front Desk see every calendar and Managers also account information ([Square: permissions](https://square.com/help/us/en/article/5822)), and Fresha's calendar opens on today's Day view ([Fresha: calendar display](https://www.fresha.com/help-center/knowledge-base/calendar/16-manage-your-calendar-display-settings-)). The common pattern is one home whose sections are filtered by the same permissions that guard reports and payments, not a separate dashboard per role.

For **customers**, the best evidence is by analogy. Baymard's study of 1,102 users found that tracking open orders, mainly status and arrival date, is the single most important self-service feature, and that a requested change such as a cancellation should stay visible on the status page, since 28% went to their email to check ([Baymard: self-service](https://baymard.com/research/self-service); [Baymard: cancellation state](https://baymard.com/research-articles/cancellation-requested-order-state)). For a booking, that means the next visit, its status and its actions come first. No official source describes the Mindbody, Fresha or Vagaro customer home, so this recommendation is an inference from e-commerce research.

Empty states have firm rules. An empty area says why it is empty, what will appear, and offers one action ([NN/g: empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/)); titles are positive ("Start by adding..."), the empty state replaces the element so screen readers do not read an empty table, and text-only empty states beat repeated illustrations when many widgets are empty ([Carbon: empty states](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)); a call to action is one or two words and dismissible ([Atlassian: empty state](https://atlassian.design/foundations/content/designing-messages/empty-state)). The documented anti-patterns are login tours that interrupt work and are "quickly forgotten" ([NN/g: onboarding tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/)), customization nobody uses in place of a strong default ([NN/g: customization](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/customization-of-uis-and-products/)), pies, gauges and color-coded magnitude ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)), "no data" flashed during loading, and metrics shown before data supports them. Sample data needs care in a multi-tenant product: Carbon warns that deletable starter content needs a real empty state behind it ([Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/empty-states-pattern/)), and fake bookings inside a real business could leak into its reports. Tetsu's realistic demo businesses are separate tenants, not sample rows, which avoids the problem.

## Where Linear's shape fits Tetsu, and where it does not

Linear is a keyboard-first tool for people at desks, and Tetsu serves people at a front counter or on a phone, so
its design holds up in some places and not others. Added on 2026-11-22, after the maintainer asked whether it holds
up against how the booking competitors work.

- **It fits the shell and the account area.** The booking software above already shares Linear's structure: a left
  sidebar (Mindbody moved its back office to one), personal settings reached from the person's own profile, business
  settings under Settings shown by role, and, in Fresha, one login with a switcher and a role per business. That is
  Linear's Personal group above its Administration group, and its workspace switcher.
- **It fits operators best.** Operators are a small team at desks, the users most like Linear's.
- **Staff work around a calendar.** Every booking product opens staff on today's schedule and keeps the calendar at
  the center of its navigation; Linear has nothing like it. The staff app should be calendar-first, with the shell
  only as its frame.
- **Staff and customers use touch.** Front desks work on shared tablets, which is why Fresha switches staff by PIN on
  one device. Linear's 28px rows suit a mouse; WCAG's enhanced target size asks for at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels
  ([2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)), so rows and controls should grow
  to 44px where the pointer is coarse.
- **Customers are not a workspace.** They come to book, and every competitor gives them a simple, branded,
  phone-first view, so they get top navigation in their business's look, not a sidebar.
- **Borrow the structure, not the look.** Linear's dark, dense style is its brand; Tetsu keeps its own.

## Recommendations for Tetsu

### One shell, parameterized by realm

Build the shell once as a shared askama layout in the web crate, filled by each realm, with all markup in templates and the only script a small island. Its DOM order, fixed for 3.2.3 and 3.2.6, is: skip link; header (banner) with the realm context at left and the initials disclosure at right; the sidebar `nav aria-label="Main"` (or the settings `nav aria-label="Settings"` in settings mode); `main` with the page's `h1`. The left slot holds Tetsu's wordmark for operators (as #148 plans), the current business with a switcher for staff, and the business's name and colors for customers. The initials disclosure always holds the person's name, a context line ("Operator", "Owner · Anvil Row", "Customer · Anvil Row"), Account, and Log out as a POST form; without script it is a link to the Account page. The sidebar is real links with `aria-current="page"` (and `"true"` on a parent such as Account while on a sub-page), in a labelled `nav`, rendered once and stacked above `main` without script. Below `lg` the island reveals a "Menu" button and opens the same `nav` as Linear does, sliding in from the left over a dimmed page with a strip of the page still showing, but as a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`: focus moves into it, the page behind is inert, Escape and a backdrop tap close it, and focus returns to "Menu". Keep the slide near Linear's 150 to 200ms and drop it under `prefers-reduced-motion` (Linear's reduced-motion behavior was not tested). Settings mode swaps the sidebar for the account navigation topped by "Back to app", and at phone width the header carries a "Settings" back link to the app beside "Menu", as Linear's does. Leave a command menu for later; if one comes, it needs the dialog and combobox roles Linear's lacks. Give the sticky header matching `scroll-padding-top`, keep items about 28px tall for a fine pointer and at least 44px under `pointer: coarse` and icon buttons at least 24px, and treat Tailwind Plus blocks as styling only. Customers, with three or four destinations, should get the same header with a horizontal GOV.UK-style service navigation instead of a sidebar; the account pages and their layout stay identical.

### One account area, the same in every realm

Every realm gets the same personal pages, each its own URL with its own `h1`, `<title>` and per-section forms, in a single centered column of titled sections with Linear's row pattern (label, one sentence, control at right).

| Page | Holds | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profile | Name, email (changed only by its owner, with re-authentication), context line | Staff email unique across staff; customer profile shows its business |
| Security | Passkeys as one card each (name, provider, created, last used, Delete with confirmation; last passkey blocked); sessions (device label, started, last active, "This session", end one or all others after fresh login); "previous sign-in" line | Required by 7.5.1, 7.5.2, 7.4.3; NIST 4.1.2, 4.5; no-store |
| Notifications | Where security notices go (two addresses, per NIST 4.6); for staff, "Only my appointments" or "All team activity" as Fresha offers | Add when there is a second notice or address |
| Preferences | Color scheme (already remembered per ADR-0070), later language and first day of week | Optional; add when there is a second preference |
| Your data | Customers: download and delete, scoped to this business | Fresh login first; deletion ends sessions and passkeys |

Organization settings sit below the personal group in the same settings sidebar, hidden by whole group as Linear does. For staff, a "Business" group (details, locations, services, hours, booking page) shows to owners and managers, and a "Team" page lists staff in Linear's columns (name, email, role, joined, last seen) with invite, change role and remove, where removal ends that role's sessions (7.4.2). For operators, a "Platform" group (operators, businesses) waits for its triggers, as the earlier note advised.

Suggested URLs, pending #8's host decision: operators at `/ops` (home), `/ops/mailbox`, `/ops/account/profile`, `/ops/account/security`; staff, whose identity spans businesses, at host-level `/account/...` for personal pages and `/<business>/...` and `/<business>/settings/...` for business work, so that a link names its business and the switcher can keep the current page; customers at the business's booking address plus `/account/...`. A Vercel-style `/b?to=...` redirect lets emails link to "your current business" without a slug.

### What each realm's home shows

| Realm | Home, in order | With little data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operator | "Needs you": refused requests, unread mail, later failed jobs and businesses to review, split into mine and unclaimed; previous sign-in; recent activity; search once there is something to find | "Nothing needs you" with a link to recent activity; no platform charts until tenants make a trend |
| Staff: provider | My day: next appointments and classes with customer and status | "No bookings today", then the week ahead |
| Staff: front desk | Today for everyone: bookings with check-in and payment status, one-tap check-in; requests and waitlist | Same, with the booking link to share |
| Staff: owner, manager | Today, then requests to decide, then today's takings and unpaid items | Before the first booking: a short setup list (services, hours, staff, share booking link) that retires when done and reopens from help |
| Customer | Next booking (when, where, with whom, status, reschedule, cancel, add to calendar); passes and renewal; anything owed; book again | One "Book a visit" action and the business's services |

None of the homes gets a widget picker, a login tour, all-zero metrics or sample rows in a real business. Charts belong on a reports page, after Shopify's rule: show a number only once the data can carry a decision.

### Build order alongside #148

**Phase 1, the shared header (#148).** Build the layout above as a shared template with only the header and `main`, used by every operator page (`/ops` and `/ops/mailbox`), with the initials disclosure degrading to a link. This meets 7.4.4 for every operator page to come and fixes the DOM order that later phases extend. **Phase 2, the operator account area.** Move the `/ops` card to `/ops/account/profile` and `/ops/account/security`, in Linear's order, with passkey cards and the session list ADR-0059 already defines; add the settings-mode sidebar with "Back to app" and `no-store`. **Phase 3, the operator home and sidebar.** Make `/ops` a home of "needs you" items (refused requests, mail) plus the previous sign-in line, and add the sidebar (Home, Mailbox, later Businesses and Operators) with its narrow-screen dialog. **Phase 4, staff, with their sign-in slice.** Reuse the shell with the business switcher (new session token on switch, per ADR-0040), the same account pages, the role-filtered Today home, the Team page and the Business settings group. **Phase 5, customers, with theirs.** Reuse the header with top navigation, the same account pages, the next-booking home, and Your data. **Later, each on its own trigger:** a 90-day security activity page fed by the authentication log 16.3.1 already requires, Notifications once there are two addresses, Preferences beyond color scheme, a command menu, and the staff setup checklist when the first real business onboards.

### Open questions

Several choices need the maintainer or a later issue. Whether staff URLs carry the business slug or rely on the session's business as Stripe does, and what happens when a link names a business other than the session's, is a boundary question for the staff slice and ADR-0040. NIST 4.6 asks for at least two notification addresses, while each Tetsu account has one email today, so the recovery and notification design (ADR-0041) needs to say how it meets or departs from that SHALL. Whether customers ever get a cross-business view is closed by ADR-0040's one-account-per-business choice unless that ADR is revisited, and the Glofox complaints are the price to weigh. Who controls staff account data, and whether a processor may brand the customer "Your data" tool under the business's name, are legal questions. Whether operators may see inside a business is #24's, and decides what an operator home's search can return. Finally, the browser support for invoker commands and native `<dialog>` should be checked against ADR-0053's supported browsers before the island relies on them.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "what should a dashboard show" to "what does each person need to act on today, in a shell they never have to relearn." The three realms differ sharply in who they are, but the sources show the differences belong in two slots only, the left context slot and the home's contents, while the account area, its security pages and the accessibility of the shell can be identical everywhere. That makes #148 more consequential than it looks: the header's DOM order and its no-script fallback become the template every staff and customer page inherits, so getting landmarks, the disclosure and the POST logout right once pays off three times.

The other shift is that Tetsu's strict choices are assets here, not constraints. Per-business customer accounts make GDPR self-service naturally scoped to one controller, the coarse device label avoids the location data Linear shows, and the session rules ADR-0059 already sets make the Security page mostly a matter of rendering what exists. The open risks are elsewhere: the notification-address duty NIST sets, the staff URL and session boundary, and the evidence gap on customer homes, which a usability check with the first real business should close before the customer home is built.
