# Cross-business profiles research

Research run 2026-11-12 by Kurt Medley with a coding agent: how a person who books at many businesses on Tetsu, such
as an RV traveler at several campgrounds, can sign in once and fill in details once while each business keeps its
customer relationship. Four research passes covered platforms' cross-merchant identity, saved payments across
merchants, identity and privacy, and campground booking data; their notes, with every source, are in
[cross-business-profiles/](cross-business-profiles/). This is research, not a decision. Where it disagrees with an
ADR, the ADR wins.

Tetsu can give a person one sign-in and details filled in once without undoing [ADR-0040](../adr/0040-identity-and-sign-in.md)'s rule that customer accounts belong to each business. **The model that works is Shopify's: an optional profile the person owns, sitting above the per-business customer accounts, which stay each business's record of truth.** Shopify's Sign in with Shop gives a store only a stable ID and an email, then creates or matches an ordinary customer record in that store ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/commerce-components/pay/identify-shop-users)), and saved cards and addresses reach the store only when an order is placed ([Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/manual/payments/shop-pay)). Tetsu's accepted decisions already fit that shape. Staff identities are global with tenant-owned roles ([ADR-0042](../adr/0042-tenant-isolation.md)), so a personal profile can copy that pattern. Customer passkeys share one relying party ID, so one profile passkey can serve every business. And no account claims anything by its email. **Nothing in the code or the accepted ADRs needs to change now.** No customer account exists in code yet, since the `crates/` folder holds only `kernel`, `postgres`, `tenancy`, and `tetsu`. Four open decisions should take a written requirement now, because they will be expensive to reverse: the payment processor ([#10](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/issues/10)), how verticals add client fields ([#7](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/issues/7)), data export and deletion ([#22](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/issues/22)), and the fallback in ADR-0040's passkey device test. The profile itself can wait until campgrounds or real cross-business demand arrive.

## Recommendations

**The recommended model.** A person may create a **personal profile**, a new glossary term for a record the person owns, unlike a client profile, which the business owns. The profile has its own passkeys, a verified email, saved payment methods held by the processor, and sections such as contacts and, for campers, rigs and vehicles. At a business, the person links the profile to that business's customer account, or creates the account from it, after proving control of both sides in one flow. On booking, the person shares the fields that business's form needs with one deliberate click, and the business keeps its own editable copy. The profile never powers cross-business marketing, discovery, or commissions. A business never learns that the person has accounts elsewhere. Linking is optional and per business, and existing accounts are never migrated by force. Saved cards are charged as each business, which stays merchant of record and owns its refunds and disputes.

## Shopify's shape keeps the business as customer of record

The platforms split into three shapes, and they differ less in sign-in than in who owns the relationship. **Marketplaces such as Airbnb, Hipcamp, and ClassPass give the simplest consumer experience and the least merchant comfort.** Airbnb strips contact details from messages and stopped sharing even alias emails, and hosts complain they cannot reach guests ([Zeevou](https://zeevou.com/blog/no-email-alias-to-your-airbnb-guests-do-you-really-need-it/), a weak vendor source; [Airbnb Community](https://community.withairbnb.com/t5/Support-with-your-bookings/Airbnb-not-providing-renter-s-email-address-to-owner-manager/m-p/2061342)). Salon owners object to marketplace fees on "new" clients they believe came from elsewhere. One owner said "9 out of 10 of my 'new clients' actually come from Google, not Fresha" ([SortTheClicks](https://sorttheclicks.com/fresha-reviews-reddit/), weak). Campground marketplaces charge **10 to 15 percent**, and Campspot's claim that parks keep all marketplace guest data is disputed by its competitors ([Campspot](https://software.campspot.com/campspot-marketplace/), vendor marketing; [ResNexus](https://www.resnexus.com/comparisons/campspot.html), weak competitor source).

**Mindbody shows the failure mode of retrofitting a shared identity.** Its Universal Account, introduced around July 31, 2025, left clients unable to sign in where studio and universal accounts were not synced, and the fix required exact name and email matches ([Bay Studios](https://bay-studios.com/mindbody-universal-account-update/), a studio's notice, so secondary). A studio operator quoted on a competitor's blog said studios could no longer change card numbers or send resets, and that the rollout was partly rolled back ([Vibefam](https://vibefam.com/mindbody-review-pricing-features-pros-cons-2026/)). That claim has one weak source. The operator distrust has a clear root: Mindbody also runs ClassPass and charges a new-client discovery fee, so a shared identity reads as the platform claiming the customer ([Vibefam](https://vibefam.com/mindbody-reviews-reddit-2026/), weak). Mindbody still does one thing right. Only the client can edit a linked profile's name and email ([Mindbody](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/201067303-Client-profile?language=en_US), from a search summary).

**Shopify sits in the middle, and it is the shape to copy.** The store requests only `openid email`, receives a stable Shop ID and email, and looks up or creates its own customer record ([shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/commerce-components/pay/identify-shop-users)). Shopify's privacy terms say the merchant "legally controls your information" ([Shopify](https://shopify.com.au/legal/privacy/customers), from a search summary). OpenTable states the same split plainly: "Restaurants own their data, and guests own their data" ([OpenTable](https://restaurant.opentable.com/?p=80424), from a search summary). Tetsu's reason to avoid the marketplace end is written into [#22](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/issues/22), which says each business owns its customers' data. So a personal profile is acceptable to businesses on three conditions: every booking leaves a full copy the business owns, linking is the person's choice per business, and the profile never becomes a channel Tetsu sells or markets through.

## A profile passkey collapses many picker entries into one

The passkey design already accepted makes the profile cheap later, as long as one rule holds. **An authenticator keeps at most one discoverable credential per user handle per relying party ID**, and a new registration with the same handle overwrites the old one ([W3C WebAuthn L3](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)). ADR-0040 gives each account its own random handle, so Lee Park's passkeys at two businesses are two entries. Under one customer relying party ID, the browser offers every Tetsu passkey a person holds, labeled only by the name set at creation ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/webauthn-discoverable-credentials)). An RV traveler with eight campgrounds sees eight "Tetsu" entries. Anyone holding the unlocked device can also see which businesses the person uses.

A profile fixes both with a hybrid. The profile registers its own passkey under the same customer relying party ID, with **64 random bytes as its user handle**, as the standard recommends ([W3C WebAuthn L3, 14.6.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)). Existing account passkeys keep signing in to their own accounts, and the server follows the link. Old entries can be relabeled with `signalCurrentUserDetails` or hidden with `signalAllAcceptedCredentials`, though the standard warns the second can hide valid passkeys if the list is wrong ([W3C WebAuthn L3, 5.1.10](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)). **This only works while every business's customer sign-in shares one relying party ID.** ADR-0040's fallback, if its device test fails, is a customer passkey domain per business. That would rule out one profile passkey, since related origins are capped at five registrable domains in Chrome, as ADR-0040 itself records. The device test is also the only planned evidence on how pickers handle many Tetsu accounts. It should note how the picker looks with three or more of them, so a future profile ADR has data.

**Linking needs proof on both sides in the same flow.** Auth0 warns that "insecurely linking accounts can allow malicious actors to access legitimate user accounts" and asks for authentication of both accounts before linking ([Auth0](https://auth0.com/docs/manage-users/user-accounts/user-account-linking)). Account pre-hijacking worked on **at least 35 of 75 popular services** tested, because they did not verify ownership of an identifier before use ([Sudhodanan and Paverd, USENIX Security 2022](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2205.10174)). A 2026 write-up describes the same flaw in Authorizer, where Google logins linked to accounts with matching but unverified emails ([SecureLayer7](https://securelayer7.net/lab/cve-2026-35511-authorizer-oauth-account-takeover-unverified-email-linking), one vendor source). ADR-0040 already forbids claiming by email and asks for a fresh passkey sign-in before linking Google. The profile link follows the same rule: the person signs in to the profile, then to the account with its passkey or a fresh email code. Tetsu may suggest "we found accounts at three businesses," as Auth0's suggested mode does, but an email a business typed in for a walk-in proves nothing. Prior sessions on an adopted account end.

Recovery and tenancy need no change now. [ADR-0041](../adr/0041-account-recovery.md) already unlinks linked identities on every recovery, so a recovered profile reaches no business until each link is proved again. One gap remains for the profile's own ADR. ADR-0041 lets the business cancel an email recovery, but no single business owns a profile. And a taken profile reaches every linked business, so its recovery must be no weaker than the strongest linked account. In the database, the profile and its passkeys are global tables, like staff identities. Links are tenant-owned rows, like staff roles, and "which businesses am I linked to" is one narrow definer function keyed on the signed-in profile, the pattern [ADR-0042](../adr/0042-tenant-isolation.md) already uses for staff. Sessions fit too. [ADR-0048](../adr/0048-sign-in-design.md) keeps one browser token with a session per business, so a profile sign-in can open each business's session when the person first visits it. No business ever sees the person's other links, so the Lee Park fixture still passes.

## Saved cards live at the processor, charged as each business

**Stripe supports this directly, through cloning.** The platform saves a card once on its own Customer with a SetupIntent ("You only need to set up each payment method once"). It then clones the PaymentMethod into a connected account and makes a direct charge there ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges-multiple-accounts)). Stripe recommends direct charges for platforms whose businesses "transact directly with their customers, who are often unaware of your platform's existence," naming Shopify. Direct charges also put refunds and chargebacks on the business's balance ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges)). Destination charges, even with `on_behalf_of`, debit disputes from the platform ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges)). So direct charges match a business that keeps its customer relationship.

The fine print shapes #10's requirements. Cloning supports only `card` and `us_bank_account`. A clone "isn't linked to, or kept in sync with" the platform's copy, so one-shot clones per charge stay current, while a long-lived copy for a subscription must be updated by Tetsu ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges-multiple-accounts)). A platform in one country may not trigger the strong customer authentication that a connected account in another country needs ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/direct-charges-multiple-accounts)). Direct charges "aren't recommended for legacy v1 Express and Custom accounts" ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges)). ACH clones need terms telling the customer that their authorization "extends to connected accounts on your platform" ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/payment-method-connect-support)).

Other processors vary. **Square** has a shared card on file used in other sellers' accounts, and warns that linking cards without permission "can result in your application being disabled without notice" ([Square](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/cards-api/walkthrough-shared-card)). **Braintree's** Shared Vault depends on OAuth, which is "in closed beta in production" ([Braintree](https://developer.paypal.com/braintree/docs/guides/extend/oauth/shared-vault/)), a real adoption risk. **Adyen** shares tokens across merchant accounts through Token Groups, enabled by its support team ([Adyen](https://docs.adyen.com/online-payments/tokenization)). Its classic platform model, though, processes payments through the platform's own merchant account ([Adyen](https://docs.adyen.com/classic-platforms/account-structure)). **Stripe Link** is a wallet that Stripe owns, recognized across Stripe merchants by a one-time passcode ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/link/payment-element-link)). It is marked US only, and its support under `on_behalf_of` appeared as private preview in a search summary ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/link/link-payment-methods)). Link complements a profile but cannot be one, since Tetsu neither sees nor controls that identity.

**A profile card is bound to one processor, and that strains #10's own goal.** [#10](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/issues/10) says processor lock-in is a top competitor complaint, and Anvil Row needs its stored cards to move between processors as tokens. The master token sits in Tetsu's platform account at one processor. A business on a second processor cannot charge it, and moving it is a person's choice, not a business's export. The payments port should treat a profile card as one processor's token and fall back to entry or a device wallet elsewhere. Apple Pay and Google Pay already avoid re-entry at every business with no platform-stored card, because the wallet lives on the device. That is this report's inference, and Connect support for wallets per charge type was not checked. #10 already notes that Stripe requires registering each domain that shows a wallet ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/payment-methods/pmd-registration)), which one main domain keeps simple.

Consent is where the evidence is weakest. Processor guides say card network rules require express, informed consent before storing a card, a record of it, and a flagged initial transaction ([Checkout.com](https://support.checkout.com/hc/en-us/articles/34282256010642-What-is-Visa-s-stored-credential-framework); [Braintree](https://origin-www.produswest2.braintreepayments.com/blog/visa-stored-credential-framework-processing-improvements-for-vaulted-cards/)). The Visa and Mastercard texts themselves were not retrieved. A safe default follows. At save, the person agrees that the card may be used for bookings at businesses on Tetsu. At each new business, the person picks the card at checkout, so no charge happens without them present. Off-session charges, such as Juniper Lane's forfeited deposits, rest on that business's own agreement shown at booking. None of this widens PCI scope. Card entry stays in the processor's fields, and since PCI DSS v4.0.1 an embedded form needs confirmation that the page resists script attacks to qualify for SAQ A ([PCI SSC](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/faq-clarifies-new-saq-a-eligibility-criteria-for-e-commerce-merchants)).

## Campers share equipment facts while businesses keep notes, waivers, and bans

**Campground bookings need far more physical data than a class or a haircut, and a mismatch can void the stay.** Yosemite warns, "If you arrive with different equipment than allowed in the site you reserved, we will not be able to accommodate you" ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/campingfaq.htm), from a search summary). Sites carry length limits per equipment type ([Recreation.gov](https://recreation.gov/camping/campsites/88681), from a search summary), amperage classes ([KOA](https://koa.com/campgrounds/jackson/site-type/rv-camping-sites/), from a search summary), and vehicle counts. Parks also disagree on what "length" means. Hipcamp counts the tow vehicle ([Hipcamp](https://support.hipcamp.com/hc/en-us/articles/32224213213076-How-is-vehicle-length-calculated), from a search summary), while a KOA park says "No need to include your tow vehicle" ([KOA](https://koa.com/campgrounds/jackson/site-type/rv-camping-sites/)). **So a profile must store raw parts (rig length, tow vehicle length, slide-outs) and let each business compute its own number.** That is a requirement for [#7](https://github.com/heavymedl-labs/tetsu/issues/7), which already lists custom client fields as a force, and for the future Clients module. A free-text "RV length" field cannot be filled from a profile later.

Demand for saving a rig once is proven, but every platform keeps its own copy. Hipcamp saves RV type and length "so you only have to enter them once" ([Hipcamp](https://www.hipcamp.com/journal/camping/why-rvers-use-hipcamp)), and Campendium saves several RVs ([Campendium](https://support.campendium.com/hc/en-us/articles/49126540970772-Can-I-Save-Multiple-RVs-on-My-Profile)). ReserveCalifornia keeps plates on the profile but makes campers type a new one each booking ([ReserveCalifornia](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=29676)). No source showed a camper-owned rig profile shared across independent parks on different software. Parks still need their own editable copy, since staff fix rig details at check-in ([Firefly](https://fireflyreservations.com/knowledge-base/guest-profile?hsLang=en); [Campspot](https://support.campspot.com/booking-guest-profile), from a search summary).

| Item | Personal profile | Chosen per booking | Business's record only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name, email, phone, emergency contact | Yes | | Its copy, made at sharing |
| Rigs, tow vehicles, plates, pets | Yes, several of each | Which come | Its copy, plus fees |
| Health notes, injuries, allergies | Only with stricter consent | | Its copy |
| Dates, party, site preference | | Yes | |
| Internal notes, ratings, bans | Never | | Yes |
| Waivers, credits, memberships, color formulas | Never | | Yes, per business and version |

The table follows Firefly's guest profile, which hides internal notes from guests and lets staff ban a guest from online booking ([Firefly](https://fireflyreservations.com/knowledge-base/guest-profile?hsLang=en)). Waivers stay with the business because each is signed to one business and one version, as [ADR-0041](../adr/0041-account-recovery.md)'s locks already assume. **Campgrounds are the lower-risk first test.** Their data is mostly equipment, while fitness injuries and salon allergies are closer to health data ([salon guide](https://salon-management-software.foretheta.workers.dev/blog/how-to-manage-salon-clients-software), weak third-party blog).

The legal roles favor this split. A host that stores data "solely on behalf of its customers" is a processor ([EDPB Guidelines 07/2020](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)), which is Tetsu's role for business records today. Tetsu decides why and how a personal profile is kept, so it would be the profile's controller, with its own notice and lawful basis. When the person sends fields to a business, the EDPB treats an exchange "without jointly determined purposes" as a transfer "between separate controllers." Its travel agency example becomes joint control only if the parties share data for joint marketing ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)). That is one more reason to rule out cross-business marketing. Tetsu must also never fill a profile from a business's records, since a processor that sets its own purposes becomes a controller for them (Article 28(10), [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf)). Under California law, a disclosure the consumer directs is not a sale or a share ([CCPA 1798.140(ad), (ah)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/cppa_act.pdf)). The interaction must be deliberate, though, and hovering or closing content does not count ([CCPA 1798.140(s)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/cppa_act.pdf)). A secondary source adds that the exception holds only if the recipient does not sell the data ([TrueVault](https://knowledge.truevault.com/article/222-do-you-sell-personal-information)). Hence #22's requirement: deleting a profile cuts its links but leaves each business's copy, a business deleting a customer leaves the profile, and each business's export includes shared fields as its own data.

The simplest consent flow follows from these rules, though no usability study backs it. On a business's form, "Fill from my profile" names the business and lists the fields it will receive. Submitting is the deliberate act. Tetsu remembers the choice per business and asks again only for a new field. A "Businesses you share with" page, like Mindbody's "Places You Go" ([Bay Studios](https://bay-studios.com/mindbody-universal-account-update/)), lists every link with an unlink button. Profile edits reach a business only if the person asks, the opposite of Mindbody's automatic sync ([Mindbody](https://support.mindbodyonline.com/s/article/Consumer-Identity-Overview?language=en_US), from a search summary).

## Conclusion

The question looked like a choice between ADR-0040's per-business accounts and a shared login, but it is not one. The accepted design keeps accounts per business and puts customer passkeys under one shared domain. That is exactly the base a person-owned layer needs, so the profile is an addition, not a reversal of #9's lesson from Mindbody. What would make it expensive later is not in sign-in at all. It sits in three undecided issues: a processor without a platform vault, client fields stored as free text, and a deletion design that assumes Tetsu is only ever a processor. Writing one requirement into each now costs a sentence. Discovering the gap after businesses hold real cards and records costs a migration.

The profile also carries a cost worth naming. It makes Tetsu a data controller and a consumer brand for the first time, and it makes one identity a target that reaches every linked business. Those are reasons to build it when campgrounds or measured overlap justify it, starting with equipment data and saved cards, not health notes.

## Gaps and open questions

The card network rules were read only through processor guides. Whether one consent given at the platform covers charges by several merchants of record is unconfirmed, and it is the question to put to the chosen processor and acquirer under #10. Stripe's docs do not say how a clone's stored-credential identifiers carry into a connected account's charges. Whether Stripe's Accounts v2 changes cloning, and how Apple Pay and Google Pay behave per charge type, were not checked. Who completes the PCI questionnaire when the business is merchant of record but Tetsu hosts the page is also unknown.

How iOS, Android, and the password managers sort and show many passkeys under one relying party ID has no source, so ADR-0040's device test is the evidence to wait for. No primary source exists on Mindbody's 2025 rollback or its causes. Merchant objections to Sign in with Shop or Link were not found, nor any opt-out rates. GDPR Articles 17(2) and 19 on telling recipients about erasure were not read, and whether fitness injuries or salon allergies count as health data under US state laws is open.

Three product questions remain for the maintainer. Does overlap between fitness and salon businesses justify a profile at all, or is it a campground feature? Should the profile carry a visible Tetsu brand, which makes Tetsu a consumer name beside each business's own? And should a business be able to turn profile sign-in off, as merchants can turn off Stripe Link ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/link/payment-element-link))?
